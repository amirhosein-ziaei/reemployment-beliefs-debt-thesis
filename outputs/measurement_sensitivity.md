## What each limitation does (and does not) explain

**1. No debt-ownership indicator (S1).** Q30new is asked of everyone. 3,098 persons (20% of
analysis persons; 2,127 of them observed more than once) answer 0 in every month. These are plausibly non-borrowers, but they also
contribute no within-person outcome variation. Dropping them leaves the within estimate
unchanged. The null is not caused by non-borrowers diluting the outcome. *Caveat:* answering
0 every month is only a proxy; borrowers who are always sure they will pay are also dropped,
and occasional-zero non-borrowers are kept.

**2. Focal answers at 0, 50, 100 (S2a, S2b).**
* Removing 50s ("don't know" style answers) or Q22new endpoints barely moves the estimate:
  −0.04 and −0.07.
* Heaping does not hide a positive within relation.

**3. Large month-to-month swings in job-finding beliefs (S3a–S3c).** This is the one
limitation that leaves the conclusion open.
* Dropping the 5% of rows with ≥ 50pp swings changes nothing (S3a).
* **Pooled:** instrumenting each belief with its own previous-month value raises the pooled
  coefficient from 0.25 to 0.33 (S3b). That is consistent with modest attenuation (about a
  quarter) from serially uncorrelated reporting noise. Even the corrected pooled estimate is
  under a fifth of the benchmark.
* **Within person (S3c):** the noise-corrected first-difference estimate is **+0.83pp**, with
  a CI of **[−0.58, +2.24]**. Its first stage is much weaker (F ≈ 30), because the identifying
  variation is the mean reversion of beliefs over two months. This estimate cannot exclude
  zero, and it cannot exclude the 2pp benchmark.
* **Assumptions behind S3c:** reporting errors uncorrelated across months, and the t−2 belief
  affecting the change in Q30new only through the change in beliefs. A transitory pessimism
  episode at t−2 that also moves Q30new would violate the second.
* So the within-person null holds for **reported** beliefs. For the beliefs behind the
  reports, if the noise is classical, the data are **not informative enough** to separate a
  null from a meaningful effect.

**4. Changes in employment eligibility (S4a, S4b).** People who move in and out of employee
status are not driving the result. The always-eligible cohort gives −0.02, and widening to the
questionnaire universe gives −0.04.

**5. Short and rotating panels (S5a, S5b).** Restricting to persons with ≥ 6 months, or
weighting each person equally, gives −0.04 and −0.06. Short panels do not distort the result.

**6. Attrition and missing next-month responses (S6a, S6b).**
* Attrition is selective on payment distress: +0.7pp more non-response per 10pp of Q30new,
  against a mean of 15.7%.
* It is only weakly related to the two labour beliefs: −0.2pp each per 10pp.
* Restricting to rows with a next-month response leaves the estimate unchanged (−0.04).
* The prediction results describe stayers, who are slightly less distressed.

**7. Common pessimism (S7a, S7b).**
* Adding expected US unemployment and the respondent's past and expected household finances
  makes the within estimate slightly more negative: −0.08 [−0.13, −0.03].
* A *future* finding belief (t+1) has no association with current Q30new: +0.01 [−0.05, 0.06].
* So there is no sign of a shared month-to-month "mood" factor moving both answers together
  within person.
* The positive *pooled* association (Model A) disappears with person FE. That is what a
  stable, person-level pessimism or financial-position component would produce. Common
  pessimism therefore plausibly explains the cross-sectional association, while the within
  estimate has nothing left to explain.

## Summary

| Limitation | Could it explain a weak relationship? |
| - | - |
| No debt indicator | No (S1 unchanged) |
| Heaping at 0/50/100 | No (S2a, S2b unchanged) |
| Large swings in Q22new | Not by outlier rows (S3a). **Classical reporting noise could**: the within IV (S3c) is imprecise and centred above zero |
| Eligibility changes | No (S4a, S4b) |
| Short or rotating panels | No (S5a, S5b) |
| Attrition | Unlikely for the association (S6a). It does select the prediction sample on Q30 |
| Common pessimism | Explains the pooled association; not a reason for the within null (S7a, S7b) |
