## Setup

* **Outcome:** `Q30new`, percent chance of missing a minimum debt payment in the next
  **3 months**.
* **Main regressor:** `fd10 = (100 − Q22new)/10`. One unit is a **10pp lower** stated
  probability of finding an acceptable job within 3 months *if the job were lost this month*.
  It is a belief about reemployment difficulty, not an observed or expected unemployment
  duration.
* **Control:** `loss10 = Q13new/10`, the percent chance of losing the job in the next
  **12 months**, per 10pp.
* **Coefficients** are pp of Q30new per 10pp of belief.
* **Benchmark** (fixed in advance): **2pp**.
* **Estimation:** fixed effects absorbed by a within-transformation (alternating projections;
  tested against dummy regressions in `tests/test_phase2.py`). SEs clustered by person.

## Main results

| Model | n obs | persons | fd10: 10pp lower finding prob. | 95% CI | loss10: 10pp higher loss prob. | 95% CI | Classification of fd10 |
| - | - | - | - | - | - | - | - |
| **A** pooled, month FE | 108,388 | 15,673 | **+0.30** (se 0.041) | [0.22, 0.38] | +2.37 (0.083) | [2.21, 2.53] | precisely small |
| **B** + person FE (primary) | 105,648 | 12,933 | **−0.05** (se 0.027) | [−0.10, 0.005] | +1.09 (0.051) | [0.99, 1.18] | precisely small |
| **C** = B + centred fd × loss | 105,648 | 12,933 | −0.04 (0.028) at mean Q13 | [−0.10, 0.01] | +1.09 (0.051) | [0.99, 1.19] | see interaction |

Models B and C drop 2,740 persons observed only once.

**Reading.**
* **Pooled (A).** A respondent who reports a 10pp lower chance of finding acceptable work
  reports, on average, a 0.30pp higher chance of missing a debt payment, holding job-loss
  belief fixed. That is 15% of the 2pp benchmark; the CI's upper end (0.38) is 19%. The
  coefficient is statistically different from zero but economically small.
* **Within person (B).** When the same person reports finding a job 10pp harder than in their
  other months, their Q30new is *not* higher: −0.05pp, CI [−0.10, +0.005]. The CI's upper
  bound is essentially zero, so it rules out the 2pp benchmark, and any positive effect larger
  than a few hundredths of a point.
* **Job-loss belief, for comparison.** Q13new carries most of the signal: +2.37pp per 10pp
  pooled and +1.09pp within person, both far from zero. The pooled job-loss slope is above 2pp
  per 10pp; the within slope is about half that size.

## How much person fixed effects change the estimate

| | Model A | Model B | Change |
| - | - | - | - |
| fd10 | +0.30 | −0.05 | −0.35pp: the whole pooled association disappears |
| loss10 | +2.37 | +1.09 | −54% |

The pooled finding-difficulty association comes entirely from **differences between people**
(stable pessimism, financial position, response style), not from changes within a person. The
job-loss association survives within person at about half its pooled size.

## Interaction (Model C, the one prespecified interaction)

Effect of a 10pp lower Q22new on Q30new at given Q13new (delta-method CIs, person-clustered):

| Q13new | Effect (pp) | 95% CI | Classification |
| - | - | - | - |
| 0 | −0.09 | [−0.15, −0.04] | precisely small |
| 5 (median) | −0.08 | [−0.13, −0.02] | precisely small |
| 20 | −0.02 | [−0.08, 0.05] | precisely small |
| 50 | +0.10 | [−0.02, 0.22] | precisely small |
| **Difference 50 vs 5** | **+0.18** | **[0.06, 0.29]** | precisely small |

The interaction coefficient is 0.039 per (10pp × 10pp), se 0.013. It is statistically
non-zero and goes in the direction the mechanism predicts: finding difficulty matters more
when job loss is more likely. But the size is tiny. Even at a 50% job-loss belief, a 10pp
lower finding belief goes with at most +0.22pp of payment distress (CI upper bound). By the
prespecified rule the interaction is **not informative** in the economic sense.

## Calendar-time shocks and weights

| Variant | fd10, Model A | fd10, Model B |
| - | - | - |
| Person-clustered (main) | 0.30 [0.22, 0.38] | −0.05 [−0.10, 0.005] |
| Two-way clustered, person + month | 0.30 [0.22, 0.38] | −0.05 [−0.10, 0.005] |
| Excluding 2020-03 to 2020-12 | — | −0.07 [−0.13, −0.02] |
| WLS with SCE survey weight | 0.28 [0.18, 0.39] | −0.04 [−0.12, 0.05] |

* Two-way clustering barely changes the SEs; month FE already absorb common shocks.
* Dropping the pandemic months makes the within estimate slightly more negative.
* **Weights.** The SCE `weight` is a cross-sectional respondent weight, not designed for the
  employed subpopulation or for within-person estimation (BK; see spec §5). Weighted and
  unweighted slopes agree, so there is no sign of slope heterogeneity across the population
  that weights would reweight. The weighted job-loss slope is larger (A: 2.81; B: 1.31).

## Within-person variation available (Model B estimation sample)

| Variable | SD total | SD within person | Share of variance within person | SD within person and month |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| Q30new (pp) | 20.6 | 10.9 | 28% | 10.8 |
| fd10 (10pp units) | 3.18 | 1.74 | 30% | 1.73 |
| loss10 (10pp units) | 1.92 | 1.19 | 39% | 1.19 |

* There is plenty of identifying variation: the typical within-person deviation in finding
  difficulty is 17pp.
* Within person and month, fd10 and loss10 are almost uncorrelated (r = −0.01), so the two
  beliefs are not collinear.
* The null within estimate is **not** the result of too little variation in the regressor.
  It may partly reflect *noise* in that variation (`measurement_sensitivity.md`, S3c).

## Statistical vs practical significance

| | Statistically non-zero? | Practically meaningful vs 2pp? |
| - | - | - |
| fd10, pooled | Yes (z ≈ 7) | No: CI upper 0.38pp |
| fd10, within | No (z ≈ −1.8) | No: CI upper 0.005pp |
| Interaction, 50 vs 5 | Yes (z ≈ 2.9) | No: CI upper 0.29pp |
| loss10, within | Yes (z ≈ 21) | Yes, in the sense that it is about half the benchmark per 10pp, with a tight CI |

**Power.** With a person-clustered SE of 0.027, Model B would detect (80% power, 5% two-sided)
an effect of about 2.8 × 0.027 ≈ 0.08pp, which is 4% of the benchmark. The null for the
*reported* beliefs is therefore a precise null, not low power. Whether it also holds for the
*true* beliefs behind noisy reports is weaker evidence; see `measurement_sensitivity.md`.
